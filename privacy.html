Privacy: Yoto Library Check

Last updated 8 November 2026. Back to the app

Yoto Library Check is a small independent hobby project. It is not made by, or connected to, Yoto. This page says what the app does with your information, in plain words.

The short version

  • The app has no server and no database. I (the developer) never receive your playlists, your account details or your recordings.
  • Everything happens in your own browser.
  • The app can only view things in your Yoto account. It cannot play, change, add or delete anything.

What the app asks Yoto for

When you sign in, Yoto asks you to allow view-only access to:

  • your family library: playlist titles, cover images, card IDs and the dates they were added and last revised;
  • your players: their names and shortcut (green button) settings;
  • staying signed in, so you don't have to sign in again each visit.

You type your Yoto password only on Yoto's own sign-in page (login.yotoplay.com), never in this app. The app talks to Yoto directly from your browser.

What is saved on your device

The app saves a few things in your browser's local storage, only on your device: your sign-in tokens, a copy of your library list (so it opens quickly), and your chosen sort order. Using Sign out (in Tech info) removes the tokens and the library copy. Clearing the site's data in your browser removes everything.

Your recordings and screenshots

When you use "Check a player", the video or screenshots you pick are read on your device by a text-reading tool. They are not uploaded anywhere, and the app does not keep them after the check finishes.

Other services involved

  • Yoto (login.yotoplay.com and api.yotoplay.com) handles sign-in and supplies your library. Yoto's own privacy policy applies to that.
  • jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) supplies the text-reading tool (Tesseract.js) and its English language data when you run a check. Like any website, it can see technical details such as your IP address when your browser asks for those files. No account information is sent.
  • GitHub Pages hosts this app. GitHub may keep ordinary web-server logs under its own privacy statement.

The app uses GoatCounter to count visits. It records anonymous page views only: no cookies, no tracking across sites, and no information from your Yoto account.
